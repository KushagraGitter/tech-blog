The iPhone Duo Sold Out in 3 Minutes — and Nobody Knows Why

October 19, 2026 · Kushagra · 8 min read

Apple's first foldable phone did not survive its own launch morning. Preorders for the iPhone Duo opened at 5:00 a.m. Pacific / 8:00 a.m. Eastern on Friday, October 16, and within three to thirty minutes — depending on which outlet's timestamp you trust — every configuration, in every color, at every storage tier from 256GB to 2TB, had slipped to a January 25, 2027 delivery estimate. Some buyers saw January 29. The phone officially goes on sale in stores on October 23. That means a meaningful share of the people who tried to buy Apple's most hyped device in years will wait roughly three months past launch day to actually hold one — and right now, nobody outside Apple can say for certain whether that's because demand is historic or because supply quietly broke.

The timeline, minute by minute

According to 9to5Mac, by 8:30 a.m. Eastern — thirty minutes after the preorder window opened — "all iPhone Duo configurations are backordered until next year," with every color and storage combination showing a January 25 estimate. CNBC-TV18 reports an even faster collapse: the October 23 delivery window for base models sold out by 8:03 a.m., delivery slipped to late November by 8:10 a.m., high-capacity 512GB and 1TB models hit January 25 by 8:30 a.m., and by noon every option on the US Apple Store showed the same late-January date. Buyers venting on X described watching their own order mutate in real time — one person logged in at 8:03, saw a November 2 ship date, tried to grab an in-store pickup slot, watched that vanish a minute later, and ended up with a late-November-to-December-1 window before the page even stabilized.

Apple's own statement, issued the same day, leaned entirely into the demand side: "The pre-order demand for iPhone Duo, our first foldable iPhone, has been nothing short of extraordinary. We are working hard to get this revolutionary new product into customers' hands as quickly as possible, and we will continue accepting orders online." Notably, that's the first public remark from the company since John Ternus took over as CEO in September — and it says nothing about why supply couldn't keep pace.

What you're actually buying

The Duo starts at $1,999 for 256GB, rising to $2,199 (512GB), $2,599 (1TB), and $3,199 for the top 2TB model — in two colors, Star White and Night Sky. That places it about $100 above Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 ($1,899) and Google's Pixel 11 Pro Fold ($1,899), and $800 more than Apple's own iPhone 18 Pro. It folds from a 5.4-inch outer display into a 7.6-inch inner one, runs the same A20 Pro chip as the 18 Pro, uses Touch ID in the side button instead of Face ID, carries an IP68 water/dust rating that beats Samsung's IP48, and has a grade-5 titanium frame with a hinge cover made from 100% recycled titanium. Early hands-on coverage was largely glowing: Engadget's Sam Rutherford called it "Apple's most exciting device in a decade," and creator Max Weinbach called it the "bar none best foldable." The crease is reportedly much fainter than on Android rivals, though not fully invisible — Gizmodo's Ray Wong could still see it "at certain angles."

The trade-offs are concrete, not cosmetic. At 254 grams and 11.3mm folded, it's the heaviest and thickest book-style foldable on the market — 53 grams heavier than the Galaxy Z Fold 8. There's no telephoto lens at all, just two 48MP sensors (main and ultra-wide) with a 2x optical-quality crop, a notable omission at this price when the $1,199 iPhone 18 Pro ships with one. MacRumors counted nearly 40 compromises versus the 18 Pro lineup once you include things like the $229 out-of-warranty battery replacement fee (versus $129 on Pro models) and a battery spec Apple won't even disclose in mAh.

Demand, supply, or both?

This is the actual open question, and it matters because the two explanations tell very different stories about Apple's execution. On the demand side: Bloomberg Intelligence analysts Anurag Rana and Josh Christensen now say Duo sales could exceed their earlier forecast of 14 million units and $28 billion in revenue for Apple's fiscal 2027 — a meaningfully bigger number than before launch weekend. Counterpoint Research's Liz Lee told Reuters that pricing the device at $1,999, just $100 above the Z Fold 8, was "a pretty bold move" that likely pulled in buyers who'd been pricing Apple out of the foldable category entirely.

On the supply side, the more uncomfortable data point comes from Apple's own manufacturing chain. MacRumors reported, citing China's Jiemian News and a source close to Foxconn's supply chain, that final assembly yields for the Duo were running only slightly above 60% as of September 17 — more than a month before launch. A yield rate that low on a first-generation product strongly suggests the bottleneck isn't marketing hype, it's the hinge assembly or the custom flexible display panel, components regular iPhone production has never had to deal with. Apple has not issued any public statement attributing the delay to either demand or constrained output, and as of this writing no outlet has gotten the company on record about the 60% yield figure specifically.

Why this is landing as a bigger story than a typical sellout: it's Apple's first foldable ever, seven years after Samsung shipped the original Galaxy Fold in 2019, and it arrives during a week when AI-linked tech stocks were already cratering — the Nasdaq fell 4.48% for the week, its worst since June, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index posted its longest losing streak since 2008. A genuine hit product from Apple, even a supply-constrained one, is exactly the kind of counter-narrative Wall Street and tech media were primed to seize on.

The branding sideshow

The name itself became its own mini news cycle. Duolingo's owl mascot — no stranger to viral stunts — posted on X: "What an exciting Apple event! I sure hope my brand doesn't get infringed upon," followed by a bit mourning its "SEO." Samsung piled on with its own dig, calling the Duo "reheating our leftovers" and a "Sims sequel," while also, bizarrely, commiserating with Duolingo: "I'm so sorry, @duolingo. They copied us too. #Solidarity." It's a reminder that Apple launches now run on two parallel tracks — the actual hardware story, and the meme cycle that frequently gets more engagement than the hardware itself.

What to watch next

  • Does Apple ever confirm the yield number? — a 60%+ assembly yield a month before launch, if accurate, is the real headline; Apple's silence on supply versus demand won't last through an earnings call.
  • Do delivery estimates pull forward — 9to5Mac and others expect windows to tighten as more units clear the line; watch whether January 25 becomes December for new orders placed this week.
  • The secondary market — iDropNews already found resale listings for early units at a premium before the phone has even shipped; that premium is a cleaner real-time demand signal than Apple's own PR language.
  • Fiscal Q1 2027 guidance — if Bloomberg Intelligence's revised 14M-unit, $28B estimate holds up, the Duo would be one of the biggest first-year foldable launches ever recorded, Apple tax and all.
  • Whether the hinge and display supply chain scales — Apple's forecasting has historically been exact; if this was genuinely a production miss rather than deliberate scarcity marketing, it's worth watching whether the same component suppliers cause trouble again for a second-generation model.
Apple called the demand "nothing short of extraordinary" without ever confirming whether the real constraint was how many people wanted one, or how many Apple could actually build.

Either way, the numbers are the story: a $1,999 phone sold through its entire launch-week allocation in the time it takes to make coffee, and the company that built its reputation on nailing supply-chain logistics is, for the first time in years, visibly scrambling to explain why.
